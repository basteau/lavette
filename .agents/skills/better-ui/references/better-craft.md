# Interface craft

Use this for creating or redesigning a surface, or improving its overall quality.
A focused repair can go straight to its domain reference.

## Establish the direction

Inspect the existing screen, nearby components, actual content, and any supplied
visual references. Identify who uses it, their primary task, and the desired
character. Turn adjectives into visible choices: “calm” might mean restrained
contrast, generous grouping, and quiet feedback; a dense operations tool needs
compact rows, aligned values, and a clear selection state.

For an established product, extend its visual language. For a new surface, choose
a coherent direction from the brief and available assets. State consequential
assumptions briefly and proceed; ask when the audience, purpose, or brand is
ambiguous enough to change the design. A direction is ready when it identifies
the focal content, primary action, density, and distinguishing visual treatment.

## Compose with real content

Start with [layout](better-layout.md) and [typography](better-typography.md).
Establish hierarchy through proportion, alignment, grouping, and type contrast
before refining small effects. Give each section a reason to exist and make the
reading order lead toward the task. Use representative names, values, descriptions,
and images so the composition survives the content it will actually hold.

Choose imagery for its subject, crop, focal point, and relationship to the text.
Preserve those choices as the viewport changes. A product image or strong editorial
treatment can carry identity; choose details that reinforce the direction instead
of adding interchangeable decoration. Keep familiar controls recognizable.

The composition is ready when the main information and next action stand out at
normal viewing size, secondary content supports them, and narrow layouts retain
that hierarchy. Correct a weak composition before adding surface effects.

## Make the whole experience feel designed

Read [interaction](better-interaction.md) when building a flow or asynchronous
state, [writing](better-writing.md) when composing its copy, and
[accessibility](better-accessibility.md) when adding or changing controls.
Carry the same visual language through the relevant empty, loading, error,
selected, disabled, and successful states. Preserve useful context across changes.
A beautiful default screen is only one state of the experience.

Use [colors](better-colors.md) when defining or changing the palette and
[polish](better-polish.md) for surfaces, icons, and optical alignment. Repeat the
chosen spacing, radius, icon, and elevation relationships consistently. Express
character through a few intentional details whose prominence fits the product.
Use [animation](better-animation.md) when transitions improve feedback or
continuity; use [PWA](better-pwa.md) for mobile browser behavior.

## Render, compare, refine

Inspect the result in the available browser or platform preview with real content
at representative wide and narrow sizes. For an existing screen, compare before
and after in the same state and viewport. For a supplied reference, compare its
hierarchy, density, proportions, and visual character as well as individual values.

Find the largest remaining mismatch with the chosen direction, correct its cause,
and inspect again. Work from composition to typography and color, then surface and
motion details. Exercise the primary flow with supported inputs and check the
states changed by the work. Use the project's relevant verification commands.

Finish when the requested surface expresses the chosen direction, the primary
flow and affected states work, and visible defects found in the inspected scope
are resolved or explicitly reported. Source inspection cannot establish visual
quality: if a preview is unavailable, complete the code-verifiable work and state
that rendered appearance remains unverified. Report the result and meaningful
verification briefly; a creation task does not need a review verdict.
